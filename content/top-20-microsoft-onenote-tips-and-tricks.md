*Adapted from the video [Top 20 Microsoft OneNote Tips and Tricks | How to use OneNote effectively & be more organized](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I725xySvwug)*

> **TL;DR:** I walk through my top 20 OneNote tips, plus a bonus, and point out which ones also work in OneNote for Windows 10. The big time savers are recording audio that syncs with your notes, copying text out of pictures, printing any file onto a page, and bringing Outlook meetings and email into OneNote. For organizing, I like tags, page versions, linking to pages, password-protected sections, section groups and a few keyboard shortcuts. The bonus tip creates a page for every name in a list.

I'm showing my top 20 tips and tricks for OneNote. I work at Microsoft, I worked on the OneNote team for a long time, and I'm really into purple. I've got my purple cardigan, my OneNote shirt, and I've even got a OneNote cape.

OneNote desktop has been rejuvenated as part of Office 365, with the new, slightly more rounded Office look and that beautiful purple along the top. So I'll be talking about OneNote desktop, and I'll also point out which things are in OneNote for Windows 10 as well. Let's get started.

## Recording, reading and printing into a page

*[Watch from 0:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I725xySvwug&t=26s)*

1. **Record audio with built-in timestamps.** Go to the Insert menu and click Record Audio, then take notes while it records. When you're done, click Stop. Double-click the recording to play it back, and OneNote highlights the notes as the audio reaches them, so the notes and the audio stay in time synchronization. Windows 10 supports recording audio too, but it doesn't have the time sync you get in OneNote desktop.
2. **Copy text directly out of photos.** Say I've taken a photo of a page of a book. I click the photo, right-click and choose Copy Text from Picture. OCR happens automatically, and I can paste all of the extracted text right next to the picture. Related to that, you can search the text inside a picture: type a word and it highlights that word in the image. This is also in the Windows 10 app, but it takes about a day for the service to process there, whereas in the desktop it happens within seconds of pasting the image.
3. **Print to OneNote.** This is one of my very favorites. It lets me print any type of document or file directly into OneNote, then annotate or type right on top of it, like digital paper. From the Insert menu I choose File Printout, pick a PowerPoint deck, and every slide is printed onto the page. Then I can take notes on top of the slides or circle things from the Draw menu, which is especially interesting in education. The same works from a browser: choose print, and instead of your normal tree-killing paper printer, pick the OneNote desktop printer. OneNote asks where to put it, and the whole printout lands on that page. That same printer works in Word or Excel. You can literally print anything you want into OneNote.
4. **Set any picture as the background.** Once a picture is on the page, typing on it can be tricky, and sometimes I just want it to be a digital background I can scribble on. Click the picture once, right-click and choose Set Picture as Background. Now I can ink on top of it, underline, or click and type, and I can highlight that text, make it red and make it really big. This is also supported in OneNote for Windows 10 and on iPad.

## Meetings and email from OneNote

*[Watch from 5:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I725xySvwug&t=329s)*

5. **Outlook meeting integration.** This works in the desktop and in the Windows 10 app. On the Home tab, open Meeting Details to pull in the meetings from my Outlook calendar, and choose the one I want to take notes on. It pulls in the subject, the date, the location, even the link back to the Outlook item. It also pulls in the attachments, such as a Word doc and a PowerPoint, and the list of participants, and then there's a place right below to take notes. It's really handy if you're late to a meeting and want to get up and running with the link back to it.
6. **Email a page.** Once the meeting's done and my notes are finished, I choose Email a Page on the Home tab. It opens an Outlook email, puts it to all the right people, inserts the attachments that were on the page, and has all the meeting notes below, ready to send. Another big time saver.

## Immersive Reader and dictation

*[Watch from 6:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I725xySvwug&t=416s)*

7. **Immersive Reader.** It's an inclusively designed tool that helps make reading more accessible, and I'm personally a big fan because I work on the Immersive Reader myself. In the Windows 10 app, go to the View menu and choose Immersive Reader. Distractions on the page are reduced, and a play button gives you text to speech with word and line highlighting. Text preferences let me make the text much bigger, increase line spacing to reduce visual crowding, change the background color and even change the font. Grammar options break words into syllables with a single click, and highlight nouns, verbs and adjectives in colors I can customize. Reading preferences turn on line focus, which helps focus my eyes so I read one, three or five lines at a time. I can also translate into over 110 languages, for example Spanish, and have the document read out loud. It's part of OneNote for Windows 10 and not built into OneNote desktop, but we will be rolling out an update in the near future that builds the full Immersive Reader into the desktop, so watch for that.
8. **Dictation, also known as speech to text.** It's built into OneNote for Windows 10. On the Home tab there's a Dictate button, with many languages to choose from. I click Dictate and start talking, and speech to text makes it really easy to write down your thoughts without the keyboard. When you're done, click the Dictate button again to turn it off. It isn't built into the OneNote desktop app by default, but that's coming in the future, so stay tuned for updates.

## Layout and tags

*[Watch from 9:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I725xySvwug&t=564s)*

9. **Move the page tabs from the right to the left.** This makes OneNote desktop look a bit more like the Windows 10 app, and a lot of people like to have notebooks and pages on the left. Go to the File menu, choose Options, then Display, and choose that page tabs appear on the left. Then open the notebook picker and pin it, so you have the notebooks, sections and pages on the left, just like in OneNote for Windows 10.
10. **Tags.** Tags are great for organizing and for finding things later. Use the Tags drop-down to mark something as a question, or add a to-do tag, which gives you a little checkbox. You can also add custom tags: at the bottom of the drop-down choose Custom Tags, then New Tag, give it a name, pick your own symbol, and choose a font color and highlight color. A tag can even get a Ctrl+number shortcut. And you can find tags anywhere: Find Tags searches all of your notebooks, and clicking a result jumps you right to that item or page, which is a really nice way to jump around your notebook. These features are also in OneNote for Windows 10.

## Page versions and the recycle bin

*[Watch from 11:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I725xySvwug&t=710s)*

11. **Page versions and the notebook recycle bin.** Sometimes you find you deleted a page by accident, or you swear you had some text on a page and can't find it anymore. On the History tab in the desktop you'll see Notebook Recycle Bin, which is like a trash bin for things you've deleted. To get a page back, right-click it, choose Move or Copy, and move it into a section. You can also empty the recycle bin or even disable history if you don't want any recycle bin. This also exists in the Windows 10 app.

Related to history is page versions. Say I updated a page a couple of months ago and forgot important information. On the History tab, Page Versions shows the older versions with their dates, and I can open one and see that it's a different meeting with different notes. To restore it, I click to restore it as the main version. It also keeps the version I'd just been working on, so both the newer and the older version are kept. Page versions can get you out of a jam if you inadvertently got rid of old things. If you drop the menu down, you can also delete all the versions in the section, or clear out your entire notebook.

## Finding notebooks and linking to them

*[Watch from 13:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I725xySvwug&t=837s)*

12. **Teams and Office integration, and finding your notebooks.** In Teams, use the three-dot menu to search for OneNote, then right-click it and pin it to the app bar. By default it shows my personal notebooks, which can include class notebooks if you're an educator or a student. Going up to Teams shows the shared team notebooks I'm part of, and I can drill right into one, and there's also a list of recent notebooks. It's a nice way to navigate between all the notebooks you have. There's a similar structure on office.com: click OneNote on the left to reach the landing page with your recent notebooks, which you can pin. Personal notebooks are the same as in Teams, notebooks people have shared with you show up there, class notebooks have specific labels, and there's a button to create a new notebook.
13. **Copy links to pages, sections or notebooks.** Right-click a page and choose Copy Link to Page, then paste it somewhere else, and clicking that link jumps you right back to the page. The same works for a section with Copy Link to Section, and for a notebook with Copy Link to Notebook. This lets you create hyperlinks through your notes, and you can paste these links into email or a Teams message if you have a shared notebook. This also works in the Windows 10 app.

## Focus, protection, outlines and color

*[Watch from 16:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I725xySvwug&t=1000s)*

14. **Full screen mode.** If I've got a set of slides and want to focus on taking notes, I click Full Screen in the upper right and all the other interface disappears. I can still drop down the top to reach my notebooks, sections and pages, and the three-dot menu brings the drawing and inking tools back. Click again and everything comes back. This works in the Windows 10 app too.
15. **Password protect your sections.** Right-click a section, choose Password Protect This Section, and in the dialog on the right choose Set Password. Once you lock it, the next time you reboot OneNote, switching to that section shows it as password protected, and you type your password to unlock it. This also works in the Windows 10 app.
16. **Collapse an outline.** This is a nice little trick that not a lot of people know about. Hover over a big outline list and you'll see a little arrow. Double-click it and the whole outline collapses. Double-click the plus to open it back up. This also works in the Windows 10 app.
17. **Page, section and notebook colors.** In the View menu, the Page Color drop-down gives you a bunch of choices, such as pink or yellow. You can also right-click a section and change its color, which changes its pages too. To change a notebook's color, right-click the notebook, choose Properties and pick the color. This also works in the Windows 10 app.

## My favorite keyboard shortcuts

*[Watch from 19:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I725xySvwug&t=1167s)*

18. **Keyboard shortcuts.**
    - Select a list of items and press Ctrl+forward slash to get numbers immediately, or Ctrl+period to turn them into bullets.
    - Ctrl+E opens the search.
    - To make a table, type one word, hit Tab, type another word, hit Tab again, and then press Ctrl+Enter.
    - Perhaps my favorite shortcut of all is for moving an item in a table or list. Instead of cutting and pasting, select the item and press Alt+Shift+Up Arrow, and keep pressing to move it all the way to the top. Alt+Shift+Down Arrow moves it back down. You will impress your friends with that one, guaranteed.

This also works in the Windows 10 app.

## Outlook mail, meetings and section groups

*[Watch from 20:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I725xySvwug&t=1229s)*

19. **Outlook mail and meeting integration.** In Outlook, select an email and click the OneNote button to send it right into OneNote to track it better. A location dialog pops up, I choose the section, and it sends a copy of the mail plus the attachment as a new page. I can do something similar with meetings. In my calendar I open the meeting and click the Meeting Notes button, where I can share notes with the meeting or take notes on my own. It opens the same location dialog and automatically creates a new page with the details, the attachments and the participants.
20. **Section groups.** Section groups let you create more structure beyond your normal section tabs. Right-click, choose New Section Group, name it, say Archive, and drag old sections into it. It acts like a little subfolder, and you can use the arrow to navigate back up. To visualize it better, I pin the notebook picker, where you can expand and collapse the section groups. A class notebook is made up of a lot of section groups, so you can really get a sense of how these hierarchies are created, and it's easy to drag things around. This exists in OneNote for Windows 10 as well: right-click, choose New Section Group, and it works in a very similar way.

## Bonus tip: a page for every name

*[Watch from 22:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I725xySvwug&t=1378s)*

For those of you who made it past the 20th tip, this is my top secret bonus tip and one of my very favorites. A lot of people in OneNote don't know about it, and it's existed since about the year 2007. Say I've got a bunch of names and want a page for each of these people, maybe for a feedback collection section, or maybe you're an educator. Instead of creating one page at a time and typing each name, select the names, right-click and choose Link to Pages. It creates a page for each person automatically, with a link to it. Clicking a name takes me right to that person's page, so it's almost like a little table of contents page.
