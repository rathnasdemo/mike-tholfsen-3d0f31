*Adapted from the video [Top Windows 11 new features | The best Windows 11 Tips and Tricks for 2021](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y8rmHjY_-1E)*

> **TL;DR:** I show 11 new features in the just released Windows 11. It starts with the centered, simplified Start menu, which you can move back to the left, and much improved window snapping and snap groups. Then the redone Settings, multiple desktops and widgets, the new shortcuts, and the consumer Teams built into Windows. I finish with the redesigned quick settings, focus assist and dictation with auto punctuation.

I'm showing 11 new features in the just released Windows 11: new features, updates and a host of improvements. And in the immortal words of Spinal Tap, these go to 11. Let's get started.

## The centered Start menu

*[Watch from 0:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y8rmHjY_-1E&t=11s)*

1. **The centered set of apps and Start button.** This is probably the one that's been talked about the most so far with Windows 11. The Start menu is newly redesigned, simplified and less busy than Windows 10. Apps are pinned there, and All Apps is kind of like the big list in the previous Start menu. There's also a Recommended area with things I've been working on, which I can right-click to pin to Start or to the taskbar. I personally like it quite a bit; I felt the old Start button had a little bit too much going on. Sleep, shut down and restart are right there, and clicking your profile lets you change your account settings, lock or sign out.

For people who don't like change, and there are plenty of people like that in the world, you can move the Start button and apps back to the left. Right-click, go to Taskbar Settings, scroll down to Taskbar Behaviors, choose the alignment and say Left. You can go back to the center whenever you want. A lot of the taskbar settings are still there too, such as automatically hiding the taskbar, and the little Peek Desktop button is back.

## Snapping windows and snap groups

*[Watch from 1:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y8rmHjY_-1E&t=109s)*

2. **Improved window snapping and alignment.** This is one of my favorites. For any window, hover in the upper right and you get a bunch of new layout options. Say I want one bigger window in the middle and two smaller ones on the left and right: I click that layout, then click to choose which window goes in each spot, for example a blog, OneNote and Outlook. It's really easy to get a cool window alignment, and when I hover again there are other layouts to try, so there are a lot of options to explore.
3. **Snap groups.** This is a new concept in Windows 11, and it's related to snapping. Say I've minimized all my windows and want to bring them back without resetting everything. If I hover over one of the windows that was open, such as OneNote, I see OneNote and also a snap group showing OneNote, Outlook and Edge together. Click it and all three windows come back in their exact same placement. Hover over Outlook instead and the snap group is always there.

## A redone Settings app

*[Watch from 3:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y8rmHjY_-1E&t=197s)*

4. **Drastically redone and improved Settings.** Open the Start menu with Control+Escape, type Settings and you get the new Settings app: a beautifully redone Fluent UI, improved icons and an improved layout across the board. System holds your information on display, sound, notifications, storage and so on. I'll show a couple of my favorite areas that have been redone.
    - **Accessibility.** The layout is much improved and grouped into vision, hearing and interaction. Drilling in shows the ways to change text size, update your mouse pointer and more, with a little breadcrumb up top to go back to where you were. All of that accessibility info has been regrouped in a way I feel is much easier to explore.
    - **Personalization.** There are lots of ways to improve your themes and background colors. I start on the default theme, click another one, and everything updates quickly, including the dark background. There are also taskbar, lock screen and other theme options, and you can drill in to customize. I can browse themes in the Store, where there are a bunch of nice, free themes, and the Store app has been redone as well. I installed one called Color Explosion, went back into the themes area and applied it. You can also choose individual things like color, sounds, cursors or background.

That's a quick tour, and there's a ton more to check out in there, but hopefully it gives you a sense of how it's laid out and the types of updates that have happened.

## Multiple desktops

*[Watch from 5:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y8rmHjY_-1E&t=354s)*

5. **Multiple desktops, with improvements.** Multiple desktops came out in Windows 10, and Windows 11 makes them a little easier to use. Hover over the desktops button and you can have a work desktop, a personal desktop, a gaming desktop, each with different apps open. It's kind of like a virtual space. I create a new desktop, right-click to rename it, move it left or right, and choose a background for it. Desktop 2 starts empty of open things, so on my work desktop I have my web browser and Excel, and on my personal one I have OneNote and the tool I'm recording with. It lets me be efficient swapping between them, just by hovering over one or the other.

## Widgets and keyboard shortcuts

*[Watch from 7:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y8rmHjY_-1E&t=444s)*

6. **Widgets, with big improvements.** The widgets menu is right on the taskbar and opens a panel of helpful, at-a-glance widgets: weather, stocks, sports, your calendar, and news pulled in. You can search, add widgets such as to-do, tips, traffic and entertainment, and personalize your interests. It's a nice way to see things if I don't want to go browsing the web, and you can scroll down quite a way to see even more information.
7. **Keyboard shortcuts.** There are some cool ones in Windows 11 for the shortcut fans out there.
    - **Windows key + W** opens the widgets menu.
    - **Windows key + A** opens the redesigned quick settings menu. This one is my personal favorite.
    - **Windows key + N** opens the new notification center, where you can see the calendar and your notifications, clear them out, and reach focus assist.
    - **Windows key + Z** pops up the snap options in the upper right, which I like a lot and have used quite a bit since Windows 11 came out.
    - **Windows key + C** launches the new Teams built into Windows 11.

## Teams built into Windows 11

*[Watch from 9:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y8rmHjY_-1E&t=546s)*

8. **The new Teams.** I used Windows key + C to launch it. This is a version of Teams built for consumers, and it's great for chatting with friends and family; you might think of it as the next generation of Skype. The first time you run it, you click Get Started, use your consumer account, which for me is my Hotmail account, put in your name and info, and sync your Outlook.com or Skype contacts so you can find the other people. If you've been using Skype for a while, this is really easy. It's very similar to the corporate and school versions of Teams but a little more scaled down, really focused on chat and video. You can look at your activity, chat with contacts, form groups and use a calendar that looks similar if you're familiar with Teams. For meetings, I can meet now, turn on my camera, apply a background filter such as blur, and join. I can get a meeting link to share through Outlook calendar, Google calendar, or just in an email. Expect updates and improvements here in the future.

The last thing I'll show is the compressed mode. Teams runs in the background, and clicking it is a lot like Skype: I see my contacts and can start a chat, a call or a video call right from there. It's just a little quick view.

## Quick settings and focus assist

*[Watch from 11:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y8rmHjY_-1E&t=671s)*

9. **Improvements to the quick settings menu.** The little icons in the lower right are called quick settings, and they're now grouped together. Clicking opens a redesigned menu. Wi-Fi and network have been redesigned, and I can turn Wi-Fi on or off, and manage Bluetooth and your VPN. Accessibility is linked right there, which is really powerful: Magnifier, color filters and a bunch of other options. We added that because accessibility is really critical for an inclusive experience. There's also airplane mode, volume, power and charging. It's customizable: I can edit to add and remove items, and I like to add Battery Saver directly into my quick settings. The gear takes you deeper into Settings.
10. **Focus assist updates and improvements.** Focus assist removes distractions, and you control notifications and who can disturb you. A quick way to reach it is Windows key + N to open your notifications and then go to Focus Assist settings, or you can search for it directly in Settings. Turn it off and you get all the notifications you ever dreamed of. You can also choose Priority only and customize the priority list: exactly who can disturb me and when, such as incoming calls or reminders, or specific contacts and people. Only those people can get through. You can also add or remove the apps that can get through. Other options include hiding all notifications except for alarms, and showing a summary of what I missed, so that if I've been heads down for three hours and turn focus assist off, I can see who was trying to get hold of me or which apps were trying to ping me. There are also improved rules, such as turning it on or off only during certain times, or setting priority only when I'm duplicating my display, say while playing a game. There are lots of options for tuning focus assist to match your needs.

## Dictation

*[Watch from 14:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y8rmHjY_-1E&t=850s)*

11. **Updates and improvements to Windows dictation.** Dictation is built in and works anywhere in Windows that can accept a field of text, even something simple like Notepad, and it works in Word, PowerPoint or on the web: anywhere you can type with the keyboard. All you need to do is press Windows key + H. The dictation dialog opens, and you click the button again to turn it off. You can also use commands, like "delete that" or "bold that". The dialog is new, so I can move it anywhere I want. In its settings there are new options such as auto punctuation, which makes dictation smart and knows exactly how and when I'm speaking, and a voice typing launcher so I can reach voice typing in a text box. Dictation is also supported in multiple languages. With auto punctuation on, it adds a period automatically, and it even adds a question mark.
