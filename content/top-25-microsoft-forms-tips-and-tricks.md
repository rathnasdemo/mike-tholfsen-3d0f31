*Adapted from the video [Top 25 Microsoft Forms tips and tricks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtQbMdqXjxQ)*

> **TL;DR:** I show 25 Microsoft Forms tips: little gems you might not know about, new features, and how Forms integrates into the rest of the Office suite. The question types include Likert, net promoter score, sections, branching and file upload. For quizzes there's a math keyboard, shuffled questions and start and end dates. I also cover Immersive Reader, printing, summary links, duplicating, co-authoring and multi-language forms, then the Outlook, Excel, Teams, OneNote, PowerPoint and Stream integrations.

I'm showing 25 Microsoft Forms tips and tricks. These are little gems you didn't know about, as well as new features, and also how Forms integrates into the rest of the Office suite. I'm starting on the office.com homepage, where I click Forms in the lower left to reach the Forms homepage.

## The new homepage

*[Watch from 0:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtQbMdqXjxQ&t=20s)*

1. **Explore the updated, redesigned homepage.** All my forms are right there. I'm signed in as an educator, so the default is New Quiz, and anyone signed in as a non-educator will see New Form. There's Recent for recently used forms, Pinned for forms I've pinned, and Shared with me for forms other people have shared. Down below are my groups: I can show more groups and drill in to create a form for a group. All My Forms lays everything out, and I can also see my deleted forms.
2. **Switch between list view and tile view.** A new little drop-down lets you look at your forms as a list or as tiles. If you have a really long list of forms, it's easy to switch between the two.

## New question types and sections

*[Watch from 1:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtQbMdqXjxQ&t=81s)*

3. **The Likert question.** Create a new form, give it a title, click Add New and drop down the question types on the right to find Likert. Likert is a fancy word for a table with rows and columns: statements go in the rows and options go across the columns. My example is a training session where I want to gather some feedback. The question asks how people would rate my training today, with three statements as the rows and five options across the top, filled in through little option buttons. I can add a sixth option, delete it again, or add another statement, and Preview shows what it looks like, and I can go through and choose from the different options. It's just that easy to fill in a Likert question.
4. **Net promoter score (NPS).** Click Add New and choose Net Promoter Score from the question types to gather data on how likely someone is to recommend something to a friend or colleague. It runs from 0, not at all likely, to 10, extremely likely, and you can edit the question text, for example how likely are you to recommend me to a friend or colleague. In the preview it appears as a nice little scale of 0 through 10, and I can pick one; for me, I'm going to recommend myself and give myself a 10.
5. **Sections.** Sections are relatively new. If I want one chunk of my form filled out before people move on to another chunk, I click Add New and choose Section. I can give the section a label, such as future trainings, insert an image, add a description and add more questions. It's really easy to add other section chunks, and to go back and change or remove them. In the preview, a Next button takes people to the next chunk. Through the three-dot menu I can remove a section or just its questions, move a section, or duplicate it, for example to make a third one.

## Themes and branching

*[Watch from 4:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtQbMdqXjxQ&t=246s)*

6. **Improved themes.** In the Theme panel you'll see theme ideas: lots of ideas that Forms generates automatically, and some of them look like fun ones. You can still choose the default themes, or add and customize your own, while the theme ideas are something Forms surfaces automatically. Click Hide to close the panel.
7. **Branching.** Branching lets people go to different parts of a form based on how they respond to a question. In my simple example the question is which day of the week they want. I click the question, scroll down, click the three dots and choose Add Branching. Every choice now has a Go To. For Saturday and Sunday I send people to the end of the form, because I don't work on the weekend. For Monday through Friday you can choose the next question, a specific question or even a different section, and I leave it on the next question. A little branching icon marks any question that has branching statements, so I know it's there. A very simple version like this is only one of all sorts of ways you can branch people off, and there are lots of choices for letting people go back and forth. In the preview, choosing Sunday or Saturday goes nowhere and I just submit, while choosing Monday brings up the question about what time of day, and then the next question, which in my example is about what I want for lunch, before I submit.

## File upload, Immersive Reader and the math keyboard

*[Watch from 6:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtQbMdqXjxQ&t=362s)*

8. **Upload a file in a question.** Click Add New and choose File Upload. A new folder is created in SharePoint, and anyone who uploads a file is captured in that folder. You specify how many files and how big: 10 megabytes seems plenty, but you can have them be 100 megabytes or even a gigabyte. From the three-dot menu you can specify the file types. If I only want a Word document or a PDF, it makes sure no one can upload Excel files, videos, audio or anything else.
9. **Immersive Reader.** It's inclusively designed to help all people of all abilities access content and read more effectively. The Immersive Reader icon shows up anywhere there's text. On commercial Forms it won't show up by default, while in education it shows by default. Either way, the three dots let you disable it everywhere or enable it, so what you see depends on how you sign in. When I open it, we abstract away all the user interface and I can read aloud, choose the voice speed, change the text spacing for people with visual crowding issues, and change the background color for people who can't read easily on a plain white or black background. Reading preferences have line focus, which keeps your eye on one line at a time, or three or five lines. I can also translate into many different languages and translate the entire document, say into Spanish, have it read aloud, and flip back to the original language. It's built right into Forms.
10. **The math keyboard.** This is only available for quizzes, so it's one for educators building a math quiz. Make a text question, go to the three-dot menu and choose Math to get the math keyboard. I can enter an equation, use a square root, and enter the answer, using the math keyboard again if the answer is more complex, or switching to the text keyboard to type it. To preview it as a student, I click Preview, and the question says to solve the equation. When the student enters an answer, the math keyboard comes up when you enter your answer, so it's really handy, and you can make all sorts of complex math equations in your Forms quiz.

## Printing, shuffling and scheduling

*[Watch from 10:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtQbMdqXjxQ&t=637s)*

11. **Print a form, and print a summary of responses.** In the three-dot menu there's simply Print Form. It's a very popular request that was recently rolled out, and the entire form is rendered so you can print it on paper. Related, and not many people know about it: on the Responses page, the three dots have Print Summary, which gives you a summary of all the responses to print as a sheet.
12. **Shuffle questions.** Shuffling means people get different questions in a different order, so in a test or quiz you can be assured students won't get the same questions in the same order. In the three-dot menu choose Settings and scroll down to the options for responses, where you'll find Shuffle Questions. You can shuffle everything, or lock down the first two questions and shuffle the rest, or shuffle just questions 10 through 15. You choose what you want.
13. **Start and end dates and times.** In the same Settings, the options for responses include a start date and an end date. In my example I only want people to fill out the form from a set time, with the end date set in the new year. Then people can only respond within those times or dates, and you can customize them however you need.

## Summary links, a thank-you message and duplicating

*[Watch from 12:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtQbMdqXjxQ&t=765s)*

14. **A link to the summary of responses.** This is not a well-known feature. On the Responses page, the three-dot menu lets you create a summary link. Anyone who opens that link sees the summary of all the results, so if you want to share the results of your form or survey with someone, it's easy to send a link like that.
15. **A customized message at the end of the form.** In Settings, down at the bottom, you can customize a thank-you message that people see when they fill out your form.
16. **Duplicate a form or quiz.** This is really, really helpful. The Share button lets you share a form as a template. If you've created a really cool form or quiz and want to give it to someone else to copy into their own Forms library, choose Get a Link to Duplicate and copy it. I sent the link in an Outlook message to a friend. When he opened it, the top said to duplicate the form and start to use it as his own. He clicked Duplicate, and it now has a copy in his own Forms library.

## Collaborating and multi-language forms

*[Watch from 15:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtQbMdqXjxQ&t=905s)*

17. **Share and collaborate.** On the Share button, under Share to Collaborate, choose Get a Link to View and Edit. You can choose whether anyone with an Office 365 account can use it, only people in your organization, or just specific people. Copy the link and send it by email. When my colleague opened it, we were co-authoring: he could see that I was also editing, and he could add a new question with his own ideas about my training sessions.
19. **Multi-language forms.** From the three-dot menu, go to Multilingual to add languages. The default for me is English, and I can add another, such as Spanish, French, German or Arabic. I click the pencil icon to edit a language, which brings up the form, and I type in the Spanish translations. When I send the form out, the people who receive it can choose the language. In the preview, the language drop-down in the upper right shows the parts I translated.

## Forms inside Outlook, Excel and Teams

*[Watch from 17:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtQbMdqXjxQ&t=1040s)*

The next six tips show how Forms integrates with other Office apps.

20. **Quick Poll in Outlook.** In Outlook desktop connected to Office 365, the Insert menu has a Poll option. It's pretty new, and it creates an instant, real-time poll using Forms-based technology. I type my question, where do you want to go for lunch, with two options, and I can add more or delete choices. Then I choose Insert Poll into Email. The email gets a View or Vote in the Browser link, and the recipient gets a nice little form generated from the poll. They vote and then see the results.
21. **Excel for the web.** In a new blank workbook, the Insert menu has Forms, where I can create a new form. It launches Forms, and I fill out a survey. Back in the Excel web file, a sheet called Form 1 was created automatically, with the results in columns across all of the questions. When I submit a response and hit refresh, the response is added automatically, and it updates in real time as people fill it out. From the Insert menu I can also preview the form, edit it, or send it with the link ready to copy. Currently this exists only in Excel for the web, and we hope to bring it to other versions of Excel in the future.
22. **Teams.** Forms integrates into conversations: in the messages box, the three-dot menu has Forms, where you can easily add questions. There are a few options, such as sharing results or keeping responses anonymous. It posts right into the channel, people can fill it out, and it updates the form automatically. The person who created it also has it back in their own Forms.

## Forms in OneNote, PowerPoint and Stream

*[Watch from 21:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtQbMdqXjxQ&t=1260s)*

23. **OneNote.** I'm in OneNote for Windows 10, and this also works in OneNote for the web and the other OneNote clients. The Insert menu has a Forms button over on the right. It pulls down all the forms I've created, and I can insert one, edit one, or create a new form or quiz. Inserting adds a link and embeds the form interactively right in OneNote, and I can even enable the Immersive Reader there. I can also paste a link to any form, such as one someone gave me, and the whole quiz renders right in OneNote, with a link to get back to the form if I need it.
24. **PowerPoint.** In PowerPoint desktop with Office 365, the Insert menu has a Forms button too, with New Form, New Quiz, or an existing form. Insert puts the form right onto the slide. When I give the slide show to people or students and they run it, they get to respond to the form right in the slideshow.
25. **Microsoft Stream.** Stream is our video solution, and you can interactively embed quizzes and forms right into your video. In Stream I open my content, go to a video, click Interactivity, then Add Form, paste the form URL, give it a name and add it to the timeline, for example at the 5-second mark. When I watch the video, it stops at that point and pops up the form. I fill it out, submit, and continue along with the video. It's a great way to check for understanding as the video goes along, especially as an educator, because you can insert these forms at different parts of the video. You can add multiple forms at multiple points on the timeline.
